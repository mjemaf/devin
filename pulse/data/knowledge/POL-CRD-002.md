MERCHANT CREDIT RISK AND EXPOSURE POLICY

1. Nature of the exposure
Acquiring credit exposure is not lending. The exposure arises from settled funds already paid to a
merchant against transactions that may later be refunded, disputed or become undeliverable if the
merchant fails. Exposure is therefore a function of settlement timing, delivery lag, dispute window
and refund behaviour, not of a loan balance.

2. Exposure measurement
Expected exposure at default must be estimated for every merchant as the sum of unsettled volume,
in-flight delivery obligations, and the expected refund and dispute tail over the applicable window.
Exposure must be aggregated across all merchants under common control, because related merchants fail
together.

3. Assessment inputs
Underwriting must consider filed financial statements, bureau data, trading history with the firm,
delivery lag, dispute performance, sector performance, seasonality and concentration. A thin file —
no filed financial history — is not automatically a decline but requires collateral in the form of a
reserve or a volume cap where requested processing exposure exceeds the thin-file limit.

4. Mitigants
Available mitigants are: rolling reserve; upfront reserve; delayed settlement; volume cap;
transaction-value cap; personal or corporate guarantee; and reduced settlement frequency. Mitigants
must be sized to the measured exposure, reviewed at least quarterly, and released when the exposure
falls, not held indefinitely.

5. Early warning
Deterioration signals must be monitored continuously: rising non-delivery disputes, lengthening
delivery lag, sudden volume growth inconsistent with sector seasonality, increased refund rates,
county court judgments, adverse filings, key officer resignations, and negative sector news. Two or
more signals require a credit review within five business days.

6. Decisions and adverse action
A credit decision — declining an application, reducing a limit, increasing a reserve or terminating
for credit reasons — is an adverse action where it is based in whole or in part on information from a
consumer or business credit report. The specific principal reasons must be provided, the reason codes
recorded, and the decision must never be executed by an automated agent without human approval.

7. Concentration
Exposure to any single merchant, any group under common control, and any single sector must be
reported against limits monthly. Breaches require documented remediation with a deadline.
