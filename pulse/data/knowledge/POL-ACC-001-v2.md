ACCEPTABLE USE AND PROHIBITED BUSINESS POLICY

1. Purpose
This policy defines which business models the firm may accept, which require additional controls, and
which are prohibited outright.

2. Prohibited businesses
The firm will not board merchants whose primary business is: gambling or gaming of chance without a
licence in the customer's jurisdiction; unlicensed pharmaceuticals; adult content involving
non-consenting parties; the sale of weapons or explosives to consumers; multi-level marketing;
shell banking; the sale of vaping and nicotine delivery products to consumers; or any activity
unlawful in the merchant's jurisdiction or the firm's.

3. Restricted businesses
The following require enhanced due diligence, senior approval and elevated reserves: cryptocurrency
exchange and custody; nutraceuticals and dietary supplements; subscription services with automatic
renewal; travel and event services with long delivery windows; debt collection; and adult content
with verified consent controls.

4. Business model changes
The business model accepted at boarding is the basis for pricing, limits and reserves. Selling
materially outside the accepted business model is a breach of the merchant agreement. Where drift is
detected, the merchant must be restricted to the accepted model or re-underwritten for the new model.

5. Vaping and nicotine products
As of this version, vaping and nicotine delivery products are prohibited rather than restricted.
Existing merchants in this category must be given notice and off-boarded at the end of their current
term; no new merchants in this category may be boarded. This change was made following scheme
guidance on age-restricted product chargeback performance.
